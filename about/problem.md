# The Problem: Waste Collectors and Financial Exclusion

## Executive Summary

15-20 million people worldwide work as informal waste collectors, handling 50-80% of recyclable materials in developing countries. Despite being essential to the circular economy and preventing millions of tons of CO2 from entering landfills, these workers remain trapped in poverty, earning $1-3 per day with zero access to formal financial systems. WasteFi addresses this systemic exclusion through blockchain technology and open material standards.

## The Scale of Informal Waste Collection

### Global Statistics

- **15-20 million** informal waste collectors worldwide
- **50-80%** of waste in developing countries handled by informal sector
- **1-2%** of urban population in developing countries works in waste collection
- **25-50 million tons** of material recovered annually by informal collectors
- **$410 billion** global waste management market that excludes these workers

### Regional Breakdown

**Africa:**
- 5-7 million waste collectors
- 60-80% of recyclables collected informally
- Kenya alone: 500,000+ collectors
- Nigeria: 2 million+ collectors

**Asia:**
- 8-10 million waste collectors
- India: 4 million+, China: 3 million+ (before formalization)
- 80-90% of waste in some cities collected informally

**Latin America:**
- 3-4 million waste collectors
- Brazil: 800,000+, Mexico: 500,000+
- Cooperatives common but still face exclusion

## The Financial Exclusion Problem

### No Access to Banking

**Barriers to Bank Accounts:**
- Lack of formal identification documents
- No proof of address (many live in informal settlements)
- Minimum balance requirements too high
- Bank branches not accessible in working areas
- Language and literacy barriers
- Stigma and discrimination

**Consequences:**
- Cannot save money safely (risk of theft)
- No access to credit for tools or emergencies
- Cannot build credit history
- Excluded from digital economy
- Vulnerable to loan sharks (300-500% interest rates)

**Statistics:**
- 95%+ of waste collectors are unbanked
- 98% conduct transactions in cash only
- 80% have experienced theft of earnings
- 70% cannot access emergency credit

### Exploitative Middlemen System

**How Middlemen Operate:**

1. **Price Manipulation**
   - Collectors have no access to market rates
   - Middlemen buy at 40-60% below market value
   - No transparency or standardized pricing
   - "Take it or leave it" power dynamic

2. **Quality Disputes**
   - Subjective grading benefits buyer
   - No standardized material identification
   - Collectors have no recourse for disputes
   - Weight manipulation common

3. **Delayed Payments**
   - Often paid days or weeks later
   - Cash flow challenges for collectors
   - Dependency and debt traps created

**Economic Impact:**
- Collectors lose 40-60% of material value to middlemen
- $0.10-0.15/kg for plastic vs $0.40-0.50/kg market rate
- Middlemen capture $5-10 billion annually from collector labor

### No Proof of Work or Impact

**Environmental Contribution:**
- Waste collectors prevent 10-15 million tons of CO2 annually
- Equivalent to taking 2-3 million cars off the road
- Critical to achieving UN Sustainable Development Goals
- Enable circular economy in regions without formal systems

**Yet They Cannot Prove It:**
- No records of materials collected
- No verification of environmental impact
- Excluded from carbon credit markets worth $800+ billion
- Cannot access climate finance or EPR compliance payments

**What They Miss:**
- $5-20 per ton in carbon credits
- $10-50 per ton in EPR compliance fees (paid by brands)
- Recognition and legitimacy
- Potential for 50-100% income increase

## The Poverty Trap

### Daily Earnings Reality

**Average Collector Income:**
- $1-3 per day in most developing countries
- Below poverty line in every country surveyed
- Less than minimum wage (where it exists)
- No benefits, insurance, or safety net

**Breakdown by Material (per kg):**
| Material | Collector Gets | Market Value | % Captured |
|----------|----------------|--------------|------------|
| PET Plastic | $0.10-0.15 | $0.40-0.50 | 25-30% |
| Cardboard | $0.05-0.08 | $0.15-0.25 | 33-40% |
| Aluminum | $0.50-0.80 | $1.50-2.00 | 33-40% |
| Steel | $0.08-0.12 | $0.25-0.35 | 32-40% |

**Cost of Living:**
- Must collect 10-20 kg daily just for food
- Cannot afford transportation ($1-2/day)
- Walk 10-20 km daily carrying heavy loads
- Must work 6-7 days/week with no vacation

### Health and Safety Risks

**Physical Hazards:**
- No protective equipment (gloves, masks, boots)
- Exposure to hazardous waste and medical waste
- Risk of cuts, infections, and diseases
- Musculoskeletal injuries from heavy lifting
- Heat stress and dehydration

**Health Statistics:**
- 70% suffer from respiratory issues
- 50% have skin infections or conditions
- 40% experience chronic back/joint pain
- Life expectancy 10-15 years lower than average

**Healthcare Access:**
- No health insurance
- Cannot afford medical treatment
- Work through illness (no income otherwise)
- Long-term health issues from exposure

### Social Stigma and Exclusion

**Societal Perception:**
- Seen as "dirty" or "inferior" work
- Children of collectors face discrimination
- Excluded from community activities
- Low social status limits opportunities

**Consequences:**
- Children drop out of school (50% rate)
- Difficult to transition to other work
- Intergenerational poverty cycle
- Mental health impacts from stigma

## Why Traditional Solutions Fail

### 1. Microfinance Doesn't Reach Them

**Barriers:**
- Still require identification and credit history
- Group lending models don't fit mobile work
- Interest rates still high (20-40% APR)
- No understanding of waste collection income patterns

### 2. Cooperatives Have Limitations

**Challenges:**
- Require organizational capacity and literacy
- Often captured by traditional power structures
- Limited market access beyond local middlemen
- Difficult to scale across regions

**Success Rate:**
- Only 10-15% of collectors join cooperatives
- Many cooperatives still sell to same middlemen
- Internal governance challenges

### 3. Government Programs Are Insufficient

**Reality:**
- Focus on formalization, not financial inclusion
- Require documentation most collectors lack
- Top-down approaches ignore collector needs
- Underfunded and poorly implemented

**Examples:**
- ID programs: 60% of collectors still can't access
- Pension schemes: < 5% participation
- Skill training: Doesn't address core income issues

### 4. Corporate CSR Is Not Systemic

**Limitations:**
- Pilot projects don't scale
- Charity model, not sustainable business
- Don't address root causes
- Often focused on brand image, not impact

## The Market Failure

### Why The Free Market Doesn't Solve This

1. **Information Asymmetry**
   - Collectors have no access to market prices
   - No standardized material grading
   - Middlemen benefit from opacity

2. **Power Imbalance**
   - Collectors have no bargaining power
   - Must sell daily for survival (no storage)
   - No alternatives to local buyers

3. **Transaction Costs**
   - Traditional banking too expensive for small transactions
   - Cash handling risk and inefficiency
   - Geographic barriers to larger markets

4. **Missing Infrastructure**
   - No material identification standards
   - No verification systems for quality
   - No connection between collectors and end buyers

5. **Externalities Not Priced**
   - Environmental impact not compensated
   - Carbon benefits not monetized
   - Social costs (health, poverty) not addressed

- Fair pricing without middlemen

- Standards that enable interoperability

This is not charity. This is infrastructure.
